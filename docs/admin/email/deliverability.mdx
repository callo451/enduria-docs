# Bounces, complaints and suppression

Sending email to addresses that do not exist, or to people who report it as spam, damages the reputation of the server that sent it. Mail providers then start filtering everything from that server. Enduria protects your sending reputation — and, with platform email, everyone's — by not sending to addresses that have bounced or complained.

## What Enduria does

| Event | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **The mailbox does not exist** (a permanent bounce) | With platform email, the address is suppressed for platform email from every organisation, because the mailbox does not exist. |
| **The mailbox is temporarily unavailable** (a temporary bounce) | Counted. After three in three days, the address is suppressed for your organisation for 24 hours. |
| **The recipient marks your email as spam** (a complaint) | The address is suppressed for your organisation, permanently, until you remove it. |
| **A mail server refuses an address** (yours, or the operator's SMTP server for platform email) | The address is suppressed for your organisation. |
| **The recipient unsubscribes** | Only the list they left stops: notification emails, or one scheduled report. See [Unsubscribes](#unsubscribes). |

Bounce and complaint information comes from the platform email service when the operator uses Amazon SES and has connected its events. With an SMTP server — yours, or an operator's SMTP platform sender — Enduria learns only about addresses the server refuses outright; bounces the server receives later arrive in that server's mailbox, not in Enduria. **Sending health** says when bounces and complaints are not reported.

## When sending is paused

With platform email, Enduria also watches your organisation's overall bounce and complaint rates over the last seven days. **Settings → Email Integration → Sending health** shows them against the limits the operator has set.

If either rate goes over its limit, Enduria pauses your notifications, documents and reports sent through platform email, and everyone with the **Manage Settings** permission receives a notice. Password resets and invitations are never paused. The pause protects the shared sending service; it stays until the operator of your installation resumes it, usually after the cause — often an outdated contact list — has been dealt with. Review the suppression list first.

## What a suppressed address means

Enduria does not send to a suppressed address. If a message had other recipients, they still receive it. If every recipient is suppressed, the message is not sent and appears in **Settings → Email Logs** with the status **suppressed**.

## The suppression list

**Settings → Email Integration → Suppression list** shows the addresses suppressed for your organisation, why, and when it last happened. A temporary suppression shows when it ends.

To email an address again — for example after a customer fixes their mailbox or asks to receive email again — select **Remove** and confirm. Enduria records who removed it and when. If the address bounces or complains again, it is suppressed again.

Addresses suppressed platform-wide because the mailbox does not exist are not listed, because they reflect email from every organisation. If one needs to be cleared, ask the operator of your installation.

## Unsubscribes

Notification email, the notification digest and scheduled reports carry a one-click unsubscribe link (the standard `List-Unsubscribe` header, which mail apps show as an **Unsubscribe** button). Invoices, quotes, password resets and invitations never do: they are not mail anyone subscribes to.

When someone unsubscribes:

- **A person with an account in your organisation** has their notification email switched off. They can switch it back on under **Settings → Notifications**. In-app notifications continue.
- **Any other address**, for example one a notification rule sends to, appears in the suppression list as **Unsubscribed** with the list it left — *Notification emails* or *Scheduled report: (name)*. Only that list stops; the same address still receives invoices, other reports and system email.
- **From a scheduled report**, the address stops receiving that report only. The report's delivery history shows the recipient as skipped.

Opening the link in a browser shows a page that asks before unsubscribing, so link scanners in email security products cannot unsubscribe anyone. Remove an unsubscribe from the suppression list only when the person asked to receive that email again.

## Delivery status in the email log

With platform email and the operator's event connection in place, **Settings → Email Logs** shows what happened after sending:

- **delivered** — the recipient's mail server accepted the message.
- **bounced** — the mailbox does not exist.
- **complained** — the recipient marked the message as spam.
- **suppressed** — not sent, because every recipient was suppressed or had unsubscribed from that list.

## Keeping email out of spam folders

- Send only to people who expect email from you, and keep requester and contact addresses up to date.
- With custom SMTP, publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC records for your domain that cover your SMTP provider.
- On Enduria Cloud, sending from [your own domain](./outbound#send-platform-email-from-your-own-domain) with its DKIM records and a DMARC policy usually reaches inboxes better than the shared address.
- Use a Reply-To address that people can actually reply to.
- Look at the suppression list now and then: many complaints usually mean notifications are going to people who did not want them.
