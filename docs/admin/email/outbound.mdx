# Outbound email

Outbound settings decide how Enduria sends mail for your organisation. Open **Settings → Email Integration**; the page is titled **Email**. You need the **Manage Settings** (`settings.manage`) permission.

Changes are not applied until you select **Save** in the page header. **Discard** returns to the saved settings. The header also shows where your settings stand: *Not configured*, *Saved, not verified*, *Verified*, or *Unsaved changes*.

## How email is sent

Choose one of two options.

### Platform email

Platform email is your installation's shared sender, run by its operator (see [How email works](./overview#two-ways-to-send)). The option shows the address mail is sent from.

1. Select **Platform email**.
2. Under **Sender**, enter a **Sender name** — the name recipients see, usually your company or service desk name. The **From address** is the operator's and cannot be changed.
3. Enter a **Reply-To address** so replies reach you. Use a mailbox connected under **Inbound Email** if replies should become ticket comments.
4. Select **Save**.

If platform email is unavailable, the option says why: the installation does not offer it, or the operator has not finished setting it up, in which case the reasons are listed. Use your own SMTP server instead. An organisation already using platform email keeps it while the operator fixes a problem.

### Your own SMTP server

1. Select **Your own SMTP server**.
2. Under **Sender**, enter a **Sender name**, a **From address** your SMTP account is allowed to send as and, optionally, a **Reply-To address**.
3. Under **SMTP server**, enter the **Host** and **Port** your provider gives you. The server must have a public address. A mail server on a private network — such as `10.0.0.5` or a name that resolves to one — is refused unless the operator of your installation allows it.
4. Choose the **Encryption**:
   - **STARTTLS** — the usual choice on port 587. The connection must be upgraded to an encrypted one.
   - **TLS on connect** — port 465.
   - **None** — only for a relay on a network you trust. Passwords and messages would cross the network unencrypted.
5. Enter the **Username** and **Password**. Some providers require an app password rather than your normal password.
6. Select **Save**.

The password is stored encrypted and shown masked afterwards. Leave the masked value to keep the stored password; type a new one to replace it.

The **Sender** section previews exactly what recipients will see, for example `"Acme Support" <support@acme.com>`, and where their replies go.

## Verify the settings

Saving does not check anything. Under **Verification**:

- **Test connection** connects to the provider and, for SMTP, signs in, without sending a message. It reports the reason when it fails; see [Troubleshooting](./troubleshooting).
- **Send test email** sends a test message to your own address.

Both use the saved settings, so save your changes first. The status shows when the settings were last checked:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Not configured** | No provider is set up, or the SMTP settings are incomplete. |
| **Saved, not verified** | The settings are saved, but no check has succeeded with them yet. Changing the provider, From address or any SMTP connection setting returns to this state, and so does a failed check. |
| **Verified** | The last check with the current settings succeeded. |

## Sending

### Send email

**Send email** turns your organisation's outbound email on or off. When it is off, Enduria sends no notifications, invoices, quotes, reports or surveys. [System email](./overview#system-email) — password resets and invitations — still goes out.

### Sending limits

Enduria limits how many emails your organisation sends **per hour** and **per day**.

- With **platform email**, the operator sets the maximum, shown beside each field as `/ 1000` and in the helper text. You can choose lower limits, not higher ones.
- With **your own SMTP server**, you choose the limits.

Limits count calendar hours and days in UTC. When a limit is reached, further email is not sent and appears in **Settings → Email Logs** as failed with *The hourly sending limit has been reached* (or *daily*). Invoices, quotes, scheduled reports, appointment reminders and notifications about email-created tickets try again later automatically; other notifications are skipped.

[System email](./overview#system-email) has its own allowance, so reaching your notification limit never stops people signing in. Each address can also receive only a limited number of system emails, booking confirmations and survey alerts per hour, which stops these being used to flood one inbox.

### Sending health

With platform email, **Sending health** shows your bounce and complaint rates over the last seven days against the operator's limits, and whether sending is paused. See [Bounces, complaints and suppression](./deliverability).

## Unsubscribe links

Notification email, the notification digest and scheduled reports carry an unsubscribe link that mail apps show as an **Unsubscribe** button. Invoices, quotes, password resets and invitations do not. See [Unsubscribes](./deliverability#unsubscribes).

## What uses these settings

Ticket and incident notifications, the notification digest, replies to inbound email, invoices and quotes, scheduled reports, appointment reminders, satisfaction surveys and booking confirmations all use these settings.

Password resets, invitations and welcome messages are [system email](./overview#system-email): they are sent through platform email where the operator offers it, and through these settings otherwise.

## Delivery history

**Settings → Email Logs** lists every email Enduria sends for your organisation, with its delivery status. Notification emails can be resent from their details if they failed; Enduria resends through your current provider and allows a limited number of resends. Emails that contain a password-reset link or a temporary password are logged without their content and cannot be resent.
