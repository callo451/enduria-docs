# How email works

Enduria uses email in two directions:

- **Outbound** — Enduria sends mail on your organisation's behalf: ticket notifications, replies to requesters, invoices and quotes, scheduled reports, appointment reminders, satisfaction surveys, booking confirmations, invitations and password resets.
- **Inbound** — Enduria reads a support mailbox you connect and turns new messages into tickets, and replies into ticket comments.

The two are configured separately, under **Settings → Email Integration** (outbound) and **Settings → Inbound Email** (inbound). Both require the **Manage Settings** (`settings.manage`) permission.

## Two ways to send

| | Platform email | Custom SMTP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set-up | None beyond a sender name | Your SMTP server details |
| Recipients see | Your organisation's name, sent from the platform's address | Your organisation's name and your own address |
| Replies go to | Your Reply-To address | Your From address, or your Reply-To address if set |
| Sending limits | Set by the platform operator; you can choose lower limits | Your choice |
| Availability | Only where the operator offers it | Always |

**Platform email** is sent through a sending service the operator of your Enduria installation manages. The From address belongs to the operator and cannot be changed by an organisation; this stops any one organisation from sending as an address it does not own. Your organisation's name is shown as the sender, and replies go to the Reply-To address you set. If the option is greyed out, the operator has not configured platform email, and you need custom SMTP.

**Custom SMTP** sends through your own mail server — for example Microsoft 365, Google Workspace or a transactional email service — so mail comes from your own domain.

## System email

Some email has to reach people even if your organisation's email settings are incomplete or switched off: password resets, invitations, and welcome messages for new accounts. Enduria calls this **system email**.

Where the operator offers platform email, system email is always sent through it, under your organisation's name and with your Reply-To address — even if you use custom SMTP, and even if **Global Notifications** is off. That way a broken or disabled SMTP server cannot lock people out. Where platform email is not offered, system email uses your own configured provider.

## Making replies reach tickets

Set the outbound **Reply-To address** to a mailbox you have connected under **Inbound Email**. When a requester replies to a notification, the reply arrives in that mailbox, and Enduria adds it to the ticket. See [Inbound email](./inbound).
