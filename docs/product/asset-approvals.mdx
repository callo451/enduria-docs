# Configure asset approvals and review timing

Use **Settings → Asset approvals** to decide who approves archiving, restoration, disposal approval and disposal verification. Each request keeps the route it was given when it was submitted, so later edits never change work already waiting for review.

<Admonition type="info" title="Awaiting rollout">

Routing rules, stages with several approvals, archive approval and the asset permission keys below (`assets.archive`, `assets.restore`, `assets.dispose`, `assets.review`) require a future application release. Until then, restoration and disposal each have one reviewer selection and one independent decision. Publishing this guidance does not deploy these application features.

</Admonition>

## Before you start

Publishing requires a staff account with `settings.manage` and access across the organisation, without a customer-specific account binding. Customer portal accounts cannot manage these settings. An administrator role name alone does not override the permission checks.

Open **Settings → Asset approvals**, then choose **Archive**, **Restoration**, **Disposal approval** or **Disposal verification** at the top. You can also follow **Configure restoration approvals** from **Assets → Archive**.

People need these current permissions:

| To | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Archive an asset, or request it when archive approval is on | `assets.archive` |
| Request a restoration | `assets.restore` |
| Request a disposal, record its evidence or cancel it | `assets.dispose` |
| Decide any approval stage | `assets.view`, `assets.manage` and `assets.review` |

When these keys arrive, every role and account that can do the action today receives the matching key once, so nobody loses access. `assets.delete` becomes `assets.archive`; `assets.edit` gives `assets.restore` and `assets.dispose`; `assets.manage` gives `assets.review`. Remove a key from a role afterwards to separate who requests from who reviews.

Named and role-selected reviewers must be active staff with organisation-wide access. Choosing a person, role or escalation recipient never grants these permissions. The requester never reviews their own request, and the disposal evidence author cannot verify or reject that evidence.

## Decide whether archiving needs approval

Archive approval is off by default: people with `assets.archive` archive straight away.

1. Select **Archive**.
2. Turn on **Require approval to archive**.
3. Set up the route, enter a reason and select **Publish version**.

With archive approval on, **Archive asset** on an asset opens **Request archive**. The asset stays in the register until every stage approves; the last approval archives it, revokes its agent credentials and stops pending commands. Archiving through the API is refused with `409 Conflict`, and a person submits the request in Enduria. Two things still archive without this review: completing a verified disposal (already independently approved), and merging a duplicate record into the asset you keep (see [Data quality and duplicates](./asset-data-quality)). If an asset leaves the register one of those ways while its archive request is open, the request closes as **Archived another way**.

## Route requests with rules

Each action has an ordered list of rules and a default route.

1. Select **Add rule** and give it a name.
2. Choose its conditions. Leave a condition empty to match anything:
   - **Clients**: one or more clients, or **Organisation assets (no client)**.
   - **Categories**: one or more asset categories. An asset with no category matches only rules without a category condition.
   - **States**: one or more operational states, such as **In use** or **Retired**.
3. Set up the rule's route (see below). Use the arrows to change the order.

A request takes the first rule whose conditions all match. If none matches, it takes the **default route**. Turn off **Use a default route** to refuse any request that matches no rule; the requester sees that no route applies, and an administrator adds one. Conditions refer to the client or category itself, so renaming one never changes routing.

## Set up a route's stages

A route has one to three stages, decided in order: the next stage opens when the previous one has enough approvals.

For each stage, choose who decides:

| Option | Who decides |
| --- | --- |
| **Any eligible manager** | Any independent staff member with the review permissions. One approval completes the stage. |
| **Named reviewers** | Up to 50 people you choose with **Find reviewers**. |
| **Staff roles** | Members of up to 20 active staff roles, fixed when each request is submitted (at most 100 people). |

For named reviewers and roles, choose **Approvals needed**:

- **Any one reviewer**
- **Every reviewer**: everyone on the stage's list who can decide, not counting the requester, the evidence author or anyone who decided an earlier stage.
- **At least a number**: enter how many.

Then:

- **Any rejection rejects the request**, at any stage.
- **Each person decides once per request**, even if they appear in more than one stage.
- **Nothing passes by default.** If too few reviewers remain able to decide, the stage shows why and waits. Use a [restart or reassignment](#restart-or-reassign-a-pending-review).

Add **Instructions for reviewers** to give each stage its own checklist. Publishing is refused when a stage could never reach its approvals with the people eligible today.

Enter a **Reason for this change** and select **Publish version**. Each action keeps its own versions; **Publication history** shows the latest 20. **Discard** drops unsaved edits to the selected action.

Disposal approval and disposal verification are published separately. A disposal request keeps both routes from its submission, so verification does not pick up a newer policy published while the work is carried out. The same person may approve and later verify a disposal, unless they recorded its evidence.

## Understand which people can act

Named and role-based stages record eligible people when the request is submitted, without the requester. Role routing recognises both primary and additional role memberships.

For an existing request:

- A new person added to a role cannot review the older request.
- An assigned reviewer must remain active, keep the review permissions and organisation-wide access, and still hold a selected active role where role routing applies.
- An originally assigned person who regains valid access can become eligible again.
- New policy versions, reminders and administrator status do not add people or bypass independence.

Each request shows its route: the rule it matched (or the default), each stage, the approvals it has and needs, and who decided with their notes. If routing needs to change, the requester can cancel a pending request (a disposal only before execution evidence) and submit again under the new policy, or an independently approved [restart or reassignment](#restart-or-reassign-a-pending-review) can recover a stage. The original request and evidence are kept.

## Configure reminders and a deadline

Timing is optional and set per stage, for named or role-based stages only. The any-eligible-manager option has no automated timing; switching a stage to it removes that stage's timing.

1. In the stage, select **Enable review reminders and a deadline**.
2. Set **Remind after (hours)** to a whole number from 1 to 720.
3. Set **Deadline after (hours)** to a whole number from 1 to 8,760, later than the reminder.
4. Optionally use **Find escalation reviewers by name** to choose up to 20 people.
5. Publish the policy. It applies to new requests.

Escalation recipients must already qualify under the stage's reviewer selection. In role mode, the directory shows eligible staff; choose people who belong to the selected review roles. Publication validates that membership. Leaving escalation recipients empty still allows the deadline to mark a review overdue.

| Phase | When its clock starts |
| --- | --- |
| The first stage | When the request is submitted (for disposal verification, when execution evidence is recorded) |
| A later stage | When the previous stage reaches its approvals |

Hours are elapsed time, including weekends and holidays. They are not business hours or service-level targets. Each stage has one reminder and one deadline, rather than a repeating reminder schedule.

At the deadline, the pending review becomes **Overdue**. A currently eligible escalation recipient can see **Escalated to you**. The deadline does not approve, reject, cancel, archive, restore or reassign anything. Settings edits do not restart an existing clock.

<Admonition type="note" title="Where notices appear">

Requested, reminder and deadline notices appear only in **Asset reviews**. They do not send email, notification-bell alerts, browser push or external webhook messages. The review inbox has no unread count or mark-as-read control.

</Admonition>

## Work from the review inbox

When enabled, open **Assets → Reviews**. On smaller screens, use **Reviews** in the Assets toolbar.

1. Check the asset and review on each card: **Archive approval**, **Restoration review**, **Disposal approval** or **Disposal verification**, with the stage when it is not the first.
2. Read the phase start, deadline and any **Review requested**, **Reminder**, **Deadline notice**, **Overdue** or **Escalated to you** label.
3. Select **Open review** to inspect the retained request, instructions and evidence.
4. Make the decision where the card leads: the asset's **Archive requests** card, **Assets → Archive** for restoration, or **Disposals**. See [Archive, restore and dispose of assets](./asset-lifecycle.mdx) for the action steps.
5. Select **Refresh reviews** when you return to update the list.

The inbox shows only reviews you are currently eligible to act on. Once you have decided on a request, its later stages no longer appear for you. It is not a requester's history or an administrator's list of all blocked work. Opening a card does not guarantee that approval is possible: holds, changed asset facts and other safeguards are checked again when you act.

Each page checks a limited set of pending requests. A **Scanned page** can be empty while **Next** remains available; continue to check the next page. There is no total count of eligible work.

Completed or cancelled phases disappear on the next read. Changes to customer ownership, permissions or role membership can also remove a card. The request and its evidence remain retained.

## Restart or reassign a pending review

<Admonition type="info" title="Awaiting rollout">

Review corrections require the application release that adds the **Review corrections** panel. Until it is available, changing settings or adding a role member cannot replace a retained reviewer. Publishing this page does not enable the feature in your workspace.

</Admonition>

Use a correction when a pending review needs a fresh clock or its retained people can no longer act. It is a separate, independently reviewed proposal; it does not approve restoration or disposal.

### Check authority and eligibility

The proposer and correction decision-maker need `settings.manage`, current permissions for the affected review phase from the table above, and active staff access across the organisation. Customer-bound staff and portal accounts cannot make these corrections. The asset must still belong to the same customer as the request.

The administrator approving or rejecting the correction must be a different person from the proposer, the original requester and, for verification, the execution-evidence author. They must also be outside the proposed reviewer list, including a retained list on a restart. A named replacement must remain active, have the phase's permissions and organisation-wide access, and be independent of the requester and evidence author where applicable. Assigning them does not grant access.

### Propose and decide

1. Open the pending request through **Assets → Archive → Review** for restoration, or through **Disposals** for a disposal request. An active asset's actions menu also offers **Review corrections**. The eligible-reviewer inbox is not an administrator's list of blocked work.
2. In **Review corrections**, inspect the current round, retained reviewer rules and clock.
3. Select **Propose restart** to keep the existing reviewer rules and restart their timing, or **Propose reassignment** and use **Find replacement reviewers** to select 1–50 eligible named reviewers.
4. Enter a **Correction reason** of 10–2,000 characters and submit the proposal.
5. A separate eligible administrator reads the old and proposed review rules and the retained reason. They select **Approve correction** or **Reject correction** and enter a **Decision reason** of 10–2,000 characters. The proposer can instead use **Cancel correction** while it remains pending, also recording a decision reason.

Until approval, the current review remains actionable by its existing eligible reviewers. A proposal does not reserve the request or suspend its clock. If the phase is completed or otherwise changes first, reopen its current state; do not assume the correction can still apply.

Approval creates a new review round and supersedes the old round's review authority. A restart preserves its reviewer rules and timing intervals; reassignment uses the approved named people. Both keep the retained review instructions and timing intervals. Escalation remains limited to previously selected escalation recipients who are also in the new named list. The new clock starts at correction approval, so an enabled reminder and deadline run from that point. Old requests, reviewer snapshots, reasons, decisions and disposal execution evidence remain retained. Old-round reminders cannot grant authority in the new round. Reload an already open review before making the actual asset decision.

A restart of a named or role-selected roster requires at least one currently eligible independent reviewer from that roster. A retained verifier who recorded the evidence remains listed but cannot verify it or receive review notices; another eligible independent member must remain. Restart does not add newly joined role members or bypass a lost role membership. If no retained verifier can act, use reassignment rather than a restart.

The new reviewers must still make the actual restoration, disposal approval or verification decision. All normal holds, asset-fact and independence checks still apply. A correction cannot change submitted facts, edit disposal evidence, reopen completed work, move history to another customer or add older phases that have no review runtime.

Use **Refresh corrections** after authority changes. Expand **Retained previous reviewer policy** and **Retained proposed reviewer policy** to compare a proposal. The panel shows bounded recent history; earlier rounds and correction evidence remain retained.

## Resolve common problems

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The settings page is unavailable | Check `settings.manage` and whether the staff account is restricted to a customer. |
| Publication says the policy changed | Reload the page, compare the newer version, then reapply and publish your edits. |
| Publication is refused because a stage needs more approvals than it has reviewers | Add reviewers, choose roles with more eligible members, or lower **Approvals needed**. |
| A request says no route applies | No rule matches the asset and the action has no default route. Add a rule or turn on **Use a default route**. |
| **Archive asset** asks for a reason instead of archiving | Archive approval is on. Submit the request; it archives when its stages approve. |
| No suitable reviewers appear | Check active staff status, current permissions and organisation-wide access. Narrow the directory search. In role mode, check the selected roles and current memberships. |
| An escalation person is rejected | They must already qualify under the phase's selected people or roles. Escalation cannot appoint a new reviewer. |
| A pending request uses older instructions or timing | This is expected: the request retains its submitted policy. Publishing a newer version does not alter it. |
| A request is absent from the inbox | Check current eligibility and use **Next** if available. Phases already pending before the inbox was enabled are not added retrospectively; open them through **Archive** or **Disposals**. |
| A notice is late | Refresh the inbox. Background processing must be running for notices to appear; contact your service administrator if delivery remains delayed. A delayed notice does not change the retained deadline or make a decision. |
| A retained verifier becomes unavailable | Restore the originally assigned person's valid authority where appropriate, or use an independently approved reassignment when review corrections are available. A newly added role member does not automatically replace them. The evidence author remains ineligible to verify. |
| A correction is unavailable or refused | Check current settings and phase permissions, organisation-wide access, independence, customer binding and whether the phase is still pending. Older phases without a review runtime cannot be backfilled through a correction. |
| The request was decided before its correction | The correction can no longer be approved. Retain a rejection or cancel the proposal as its proposer; completed asset work cannot be restarted. |

These settings route archive, restoration and disposal. Custody hand-overs, transfers, long loans and marking assets lost have their own approval settings (see [Track asset custody](./asset-custody)). There is no automatic approval on a deadline, no email voting and no automatic reassignment. [Asset event rules](./asset-event-rules) are separate: when available, they can highlight an existing review for an eligible person but cannot assign review authority.
