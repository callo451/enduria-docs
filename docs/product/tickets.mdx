# Work with tickets

Enduria stores incidents, service requests, changes, problems, and general tickets in one work queue while preserving the lifecycle of each type.

For response targets, configured pauses and business calendars, see [service-level targets](../admin/service-level-targets).

## Find work

Use queue search, filters, and saved views to narrow the list by assignee, customer, type, status, priority, or other configured fields. Status and priority names come from your organisation's settings.

> **Awaiting rollout.** Configured workspace choices and queue classification described below are awaiting application rollout.

Under **Loaded work**, **My open work**, **Unassigned** and **SLA breached** include work with a recognised unfinished status. **Awaiting approval** uses the status's approval category. **All work** retains finished work and unfamiliar or retired labels too, so you can review recorded evidence. View counts describe the rows currently loaded in the queue; they are not organisation-wide totals.

### Find work beyond the loaded rows

> **Awaiting rollout.** Bounded ticket lists and selection search described here are awaiting application rollout.

Lists load a page of work rather than every ticket. A loaded or shown count describes those rows; a matching total describes work within your current permissions and filters. Dashboard and workspace summaries can remain limited samples. **Export visible rows** exports the queue's visible rows, not every matching ticket.

Use **Search incidents by number or title...** in incident selection, or **Search tickets…** when linking work to a project. Search narrows matching work beyond the current page. **Previous** and **Next** reach additional pages; changing a search starts again at the first page. The project selector's **Select page** and **Deselect page** affect that page, rather than all matching tickets. Status-page incident management also provides **Previous** and **Next** and a matching total. If a record is still missing, check the work-item type, filters and your access before assuming it does not exist.

**My Requests** shows loaded rows, the matching total and the current page, with **Previous** and **Next**. Its **Requests on this page**, **Open on this page**, **Pending on this page** and **Resolved on this page** cards describe the current page. **All statuses** and **All priorities** use your organisation's configured choices.

A failed search or page load is not an empty result. Use **Retry incidents**, **Retry tickets** or **Retry requests** for the affected list, and **Retry filters** if configured filters failed to load. Retry before selecting another record or creating duplicate work.

## Work a ticket

1. Open a ticket from the queue.
2. Review the summary, requester, service context, priority, service targets, and linked records.
3. Assign an owner or team when required.
4. Add an internal note or a customer-visible reply in the conversation.
5. Record time and attach supporting material.
6. Move the ticket through a valid status transition when the work changes state.

Changes may require a versioned review and approval before implementation. Problems can publish reviewed known-error revisions independently of the problem status.

Under **Properties**, choose **Status** or **Priority** from your organisation's active choices for that work-item type. On smaller screens, choose **Properties** at the top of the ticket to open **Ticket properties**. Names and ordering follow Settings → Tickets; priorities also show their configured level. Retired or unrecognised current values remain visible; they cannot be selected as a new value. If choices fail to load, choose **Retry choices**, or reload the ticket. Status selection still follows approval, transition, RFC and reopening requirements. Complete resolved or closed work through the resolution dialog below; bulk actions do not supply resolution evidence for you.

Administrators can [configure ticket statuses and defaults](../admin/ticket-statuses.mdx). That guide includes rollout availability, creation errors, reopening permissions and historical import mappings.

New work receives an organisation-configured number. Administrators can [configure ticket numbers](../admin/ticket-numbering); changing the format leaves existing references unchanged.

## Review email replies

> **Awaiting rollout.** Unverified email notes and staff review are awaiting application rollout.

Email replies from unverified or unrelated senders appear as internal notes marked **Unverified sender**, with their files kept staff-only. Review the sender evidence, message and files before disclosure. Current staff with ticket write access and `tickets.edit.all`, or `tickets.edit.own` on an involved ticket, can choose **Make public** and confirm to make that note and its linked files customer-visible. The original sender and verification evidence remain recorded, alongside the reviewer and review time.

Making the note public requests a customer notification through normal delivery. It does not create a staff first-response fact or change the original author. Ordinary comment editing is not a substitute for this review. See [inbound email](../admin/email/inbound) for participant and mailbox policy rules.

## Record a resolution

> **Awaiting rollout.** The resolution dialog and retained resolution history described here are awaiting application rollout.

You need access to the ticket and permission to edit it. Tickets and incidents require a resolution code and explanation by default; your administrator can enable the same requirement for other work-item types.

1. Open the ticket and choose **Resolve**. Choosing a configured resolved or closed status in the status selector also opens the dialog.
2. Choose **Destination status** from your organisation's active resolved or closed statuses.
3. Select a **Resolution code** and enter the **Resolution explanation**. The dialog marks required fields.
4. Choose **Save resolution**.

The status and evidence are saved together. If saving fails, the dialog keeps your entries so you can correct the problem. If there is no applicable code or destination, ask an administrator to review **Settings → Tickets → Resolution** or **Statuses**. Approval, transition and RFC requirements still apply.

**Resolution history** shows the code name and explanation recorded for previous resolutions. It is read-only. Reopening requires reopening permission and clears current resolution evidence without removing that history. Completing a later cycle needs fresh evidence. You cannot replace evidence by saving the same finished status again.

## Change the client

> **Awaiting rollout.** The stock-related client restriction is awaiting application rollout.

Changing a ticket's client requires permission to edit the ticket and access to the destination client. The workspace currently displays the client without an editing control. Once stock has moved against a ticket, or it has a retained hardware billing record, its client cannot be changed, added or cleared. This remains true after a return or when the net stock movement is zero. Saving the same client is still allowed.

A pending stock reservation alone does not freeze the client; its existing reservation and approval rules still apply. If a client change is refused, keep the recorded client and ask your administrator to review the ticket and stock records. Editing the ticket does not reverse stock or transfer recorded billing to another client.

Link relevant assets, knowledge articles, projects, and related tickets. This keeps operational context attached to the work instead of burying it in comments.
:::
